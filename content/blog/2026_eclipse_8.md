What an amazing experience that was! Also, we were _ridiculously_ lucky.

I wanted to mention a couple of tools that were helpful to me.

First, [Xavier Jubier's eclipse website](http://xjubier.free.fr/en/index_en.html) is a phenomenal resource. It has detailed path maps for solar and lunar eclipses -- this should be stop #1 for people wanting to see an eclipse.

Another great planning tool is [PeakFinder](https://www.peakfinder.com), which let me plot out the sun and moon's position relative to local terrain features. I used this to vet some potential viewing sites ahead of time.

[Alan Dyer's eclipse photography book](https://www.amazingsky.com/EclipseBook) exhaustively covers techniques for imaging eclipses, from telephotos to wide angle shots to timelapses.

Gordon Telespun's [talking solar eclipse timing app](https://solareclipsetimer.com/) was very helpful for eclipse day observing.
